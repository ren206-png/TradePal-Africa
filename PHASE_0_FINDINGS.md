## 0.1 Kill Questions

### KQ1 — WhatsApp platform access: direct Cloud API vs BSP, verification, per-country costs

**Finding:** Use Meta's direct Cloud API, not a BSP, for MVP. Business verification is achievable for a Nigeria/Kenya/Ghana/Sierra Leone-registered entity but requires real corporate paperwork (no informal/sole-proprietor shortcuts), and Sierra Leone is priced as part of an undifferentiated "Rest of Africa" bucket rather than a standalone market — this is a real cost-forecasting risk, not a rounding error.

**Evidence:**
- Meta Business verification requires business registration documents, a tax ID, and a utility bill/bank statement matching the business's legal name and address; country-specific docs include a CAC certificate in Nigeria and a Certificate of Incorporation in Kenya. Typical approval is 1–10 business days if the legal name matches exactly across all surfaces (website, WABA, business manager). [Meta developer docs — Official Business Accounts](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/official-business-accounts/), [zaple.ai verification guide](https://zaple.ai/blog/meta-business-verification-whatsapp/)
- As of July 1, 2025, Meta bills **per delivered template message**, not per 24-hour conversation window (the old model). Three billed template categories: **Marketing** (always billed), **Utility** and **Authentication** (billed only *outside* an open customer-service window; free inside it). Non-template free-form messages sent inside a 24-hour customer-service window are free, unlimited, uncapped (the old 1,000 free-conversation cap was removed Nov 1, 2024). [Meta developer docs — Pricing](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/pricing)
- Meta's rate card lists 40+ countries individually plus regional buckets ("Rest of Africa," "Rest of Asia Pacific"). **Sierra Leone is not a standalone line item — it is grouped into "Rest of Africa"** (calling code 232). Nigeria and Kenya are large enough markets to plausibly have differentiated or near-lowest rates given WhatsApp's penetration there, but this needs re-confirmation from Meta's live rate card at contract time, not assumed from blog aggregators. [Meta developer docs — Pricing](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/pricing); third-party aggregation citing Sierra Leone utility/auth rates around $0.005/message and marketing around $0.028/message from a Salesforce-published rate card — **treat as indicative only, not authoritative** ([Salesforce rate card PDF](https://www.salesforce.com/en-us/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/assets/pdf/whatsapp-business-messaging-rate-card-mm-lite-feb-1-2026.pdf)).

**Consequence for the build:**
- Direct Cloud API is viable and cheaper long-run than a BSP markup (Twilio/360dialog add a per-message or per-seat fee on top of Meta's own rate). Use direct Cloud API as the ADR decision below.
- Because almost all of TradePal's core loop (merchant texts → bot replies) happens **inside the 24-hour service window that the merchant themselves opened**, the dominant recurring cost is **not** WhatsApp messaging — it's near-zero under Meta's current model. The only billed messages are business-initiated ones outside the window: low-stock alerts, subscription/billing notices, and (if ever built) proactive nudges. These must go through the outbound-gateway design already specified and be flagged, not core-path.
- Do not price Sierra Leone-specific unit economics with confidence until Meta actually differentiates Sierra Leone from "Rest of Africa" in the live rate card, or a direct quote is pulled at integration time.

### KQ2 — The customer-reminder feature as originally conceived is a Meta policy violation

**Finding:** Confirmed. This is not a defensible interpretation question — Meta's policy is explicit and unconditional: **opt-in must come from the recipient themselves.** A merchant "authorizing" the bot to message their customer is not equivalent to that customer's own opt-in, and there is no provision anywhere in Meta's Business Messaging Policy for a business to delegate/inherit opt-in from a third party's relationship with someone else. The originally-conceived feature ("bot messages the merchant's customer to remind them of a debt") cannot be built as specified without violating policy and risking WABA suspension.

**Evidence:**
- "Businesses may only contact people on WhatsApp if... businesses have received opt-in permission from the recipient confirming that they wish to receive subsequent messages or calls from a particular business." The opt-in is *recipient-specific* and *business-specific* — it cannot be satisfied by a different party's consent. [Meta for Developers — Get opt-in for WhatsApp](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/getting-opt-in)
- Every message sent outside an open 24-hour service window must use a Meta-approved template in the correct category; miscategorization or unapproved sending risks template rejection, message blocking, or account suspension. A debt-reminder message sent to someone who has never messaged the merchant's WABA and never opted in directly has no lawful category to sit in — it would need to be sent as an unsolicited marketing/utility template to a non-opted-in number, which Meta will reject or penalize on discovery. [WhatsApp Business Messaging Policy](https://whatsappbusiness.com/policy/)

**Compliant MVP alternative (already reflected in the spec's command design, confirmed correct):** `/remind <name>` generates the reminder text in the merchant's language and delivers it **to the merchant**, who copies/forwards it manually through their own WhatsApp, SMS, or in person. TradePal's outbound gateway must be architecturally incapable of sending to any number that is not a registered `merchants.phone_number` — this is already specified as Non-Negotiable Standard #9 and must remain the single send path with no exceptions, including for this feature.

### KQ3 — Unit economics

**Finding:** Per-message AI cost is negligible (fractions of a cent); WhatsApp messaging cost for the core loop is effectively zero because it runs inside merchant-opened service windows. The real cost drivers for a Free tier are voice-note volume (ASR is ~10-20x the cost of a text parse) and business-initiated template messages (alerts), both of which are already flagged/capped features in the spec. A capped Free tier is financially defensible; unlimited free voice is not.

**Evidence — LLM cost per text message.** Assume ~150 input tokens (short merchant message + system prompt/context) and ~75 output tokens (structured JSON), a reasonable range for this task:
- Cheapest viable tier, Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite: $0.10/M input, $0.40/M output → (150×$0.10 + 75×$0.40)/1,000,000 ≈ **$0.000045/message**. [Google AI pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)
- GPT-4o-mini: $0.15/M input, $0.60/M output → **$0.0000675/message**. [OpenAI API pricing](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing)
- Claude Haiku 4.5: $1/M input, $5/M output → **$0.000525/message** (higher quality/instruction-following tier). [Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)

**Evidence — ASR + LLM cost per voice note.** Assume an 8-second note (~0.133 min):
- OpenAI transcription (Whisper/GPT-4o-transcribe tier): $0.006/min → 0.133×$0.006 ≈ **$0.0008/note** for ASR alone. [OpenAI pricing coverage](https://tokenmix.ai/blog/whisper-api-pricing)
- Add LLM parse of the resulting transcript (same cost band as above, ~$0.00005–$0.0005) → **total ≈ $0.0009–$0.0013 per voice note**, i.e. roughly 2–20x a text message depending on LLM tier chosen, dominated by ASR.

**Monthly cost per active merchant (illustrative model, not a forecast):**
Assume 25 messages/day average (within the stated 15–40 range), 80% text / 20% voice:
- Text: 20/day × 30 days × ~$0.0002 (mid-tier LLM) ≈ **$0.12/month**
- Voice: 5/day × 30 days × ~$0.0011 ≈ **$0.165/month**
- Business-initiated alert templates (e.g., weekly low-stock alert, ~4/month) at an indicative utility rate of ~$0.005–$0.01 ≈ **$0.02–$0.04/month**
- **Total AI + messaging marginal cost: roughly $0.30–$0.35/active merchant/month**, before hosting/DB/queue infrastructure amortization (small but non-zero at scale — not modeled here, needs real infra sizing in Phase 1).

**Consequence for the build:** A **Free tier is financially viable only if capped** — the risk is not the median user, it's the tail (a merchant sending 40+ messages/day with heavy voice use costs meaningfully more than the median). Recommended caps for Free: text-only (no voice), hard cap of ~30–50 logged transactions/month, no forwarded-SMS reconciliation, no low-stock alert templates. A paid Starter tier unlocks voice, higher/no caps, and alerts, priced in local currency at a level that comfortably clears the ~$0.30–$1.00/month marginal cost even for heavy users (e.g., an equivalent of $1–3/month local-currency price point) — this is a business decision to make in Phase 2/3, not now, but the math supports it being viable rather than a loss-leader trap.

### KQ4 — Language and ASR feasibility

**Finding:** No provider currently gives strong support across all four target languages/dialects. Standard Swahili is reasonably well supported; Nigerian Pidgin, Sheng, and Twi are weak-to-unsupported by general-purpose ASR; **Krio has no confirmed provider support and is the correct candidate for "text-first, voice deferred."** Do not promise voice-note accuracy for Sierra Leone at launch.

**Evidence:**
- **Nigerian Pidgin:** general-purpose Whisper performs poorly on Nigerian speech variants — Whisper Small showed WER **>100%** on Nigerian "Deep Cut" movie content in one benchmark (WER over 100% indicates the transcript is effectively unusable, not just noisy). A fine-tuned Wav2Vec2 XLSR-53 model reached 29.6% WER on a Nigerian Pidgin test set specifically, far better than zero-shot Whisper, but that requires custom fine-tuning infrastructure TradePal does not have in Phase 0/1. [engrxiv Nigerian speech benchmarking](https://engrxiv.org/preprint/view/6650); comparative Whisper/Jamaican Patois figures showing similar creole degradation (WER 0.89 vs 0.05 on standard English) as an analog. No major commercial ASR vendor (OpenAI, Google, Azure, ElevenLabs, Deepgram) explicitly lists Nigerian Pidgin as a supported language.
- **Swahili / Sheng:** standard Swahili has real, decent support — ElevenLabs Scribe reports 3.1% WER on FLEURS and 5.5% on Common Voice for Swahili, and Whisper-Tiny has been successfully fine-tuned for Swahili in research settings. [ElevenLabs Swahili STT](https://elevenlabs.io/speech-to-text/swahili); [ACL Anthology Whisper-Tiny Swahili fine-tuning](https://aclanthology.org/2025.africanlp-1.11/). **Sheng (code-switched Kenyan urban slang) has no public benchmark found** — treat as materially weaker than standard Swahili and expect real-world failures on slang-heavy input; no source claims otherwise.
- **Krio:** **no public benchmark or vendor claim of Krio support was found.** Krio is not among OpenAI Whisper's ~99 officially listed languages, and no African-ASR research project surfaced in this search (e.g., the "African Whisper" fine-tuning framework) confirmed a shipped Krio model. [Krio language background](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krio_language); [African Whisper framework](https://kevkibe.github.io/African-Whisper/) (framework exists for fine-tuning African languages generally, but no confirmed Krio artifact located). **This confirms the hypothesis in the build brief: Sierra Leone should launch text-first, with voice explicitly deferred and gated off by feature flag until a Krio-capable model is found or trained.**
- **Twi:** OpenAI Whisper does **not** officially support Twi. Ghana NLP's Khaya AI is the most credible option — it ships ASR for Twi, Ewe, Yoruba, Ga, Dagbani, with incremental accuracy gains (~3.5–6% improvement over prior versions) from real usage by "tens of thousands" of users, but is a smaller, community-research-grade system rather than an enterprise SLA vendor. A real complication: "Asante Twi" and "Akuapem Twi" are distinct enough that a model trained on one degrades on the other, and most datasets conflate them. [Khaya AI](https://ghananlp.org/project/translator-webapp/); [jbklutse Twi AI overview](https://www.jbklutse.com/ai-twi-ghana/)

**Consequence for the build:** No single ASR vendor covers this language set. Fallback path for all four languages, not just Krio, must be real and low-friction: on low ASR confidence, the bot replies asking the merchant to type the transaction instead of guessing from a bad transcript — never silently persist a low-confidence transcript's parse. ASR provider selection should be per-country config (e.g., a general vendor for Swahili/Kenya, Khaya AI or a Twi-specific path for Ghana, general vendor with low confidence expectations for Nigerian Pidgin, **no voice path enabled for Sierra Leone at MVP**).

### KQ5 — Data protection

**Finding:** Nigeria, Kenya, and Ghana all have real, enforced, registration-requiring data protection regimes already in force. **Sierra Leone currently has no comprehensive data protection law in force** — its Data Protection and Right to Access Information Bill was still completing national validation as of November 2025, with Cabinet only authorizing drafting/enactment in April 2026; it has not yet been enacted by Parliament as of this research date. Per the build brief's own instruction, TradePal must default to the strictest of the four enacted regimes as its baseline for Sierra Leone operations rather than treating the absence of law as absence of obligation.

**Evidence:**
- **Nigeria — NDPA 2023:** Section 24(1)(c) requires data minimization ("adequate, relevant and limited to the minimum necessary"); Section 24(1)(d) requires retention "not longer than is necessary" for the lawful basis; data subject rights include access, correction, erasure, consent withdrawal, objection, and portability (structured/machine-readable export). Regulator: Nigeria Data Protection Commission (NDPC). [Securiti NDPA overview](https://securiti.ai/overview-of-nigeria-data-protection-act/); [KPMG NDPA explanatory memorandum](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmg/ng/pdf/the-nigeria-data-protection-act-2023.pdf)
- **Kenya — DPA 2019:** mandatory registration with the Office of the Data Protection Commissioner (ODPC) for controllers/processors above KES 5M annual turnover or 10+ employees — **and financial-services-sector entities must register regardless of size**, which likely captures TradePal given it processes financial transaction data for merchants. Registration is tiered (micro/small, medium, large), certificates valid 24 months, ~14-day approval SLA. [ODPC guidance note](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/ODPC-Guidance-Note-on-Registration-of-Data-Controllers-and-Data-Processors.pdf); [Njagaa Advocates ODPC registration guide](https://njagaadvocates.com/registration-as-a-data-controller-and-data-processor-in-kenya-with-the-odpc/)
- **Ghana — Data Protection Act 2012 (Act 843):** mandatory registration with the Data Protection Commission within 20 days of commencing business in Ghana; registration renewed every 2 years; operating unregistered is an offence (fine up to 250 penalty units or up to 2 years imprisonment under s.56). [Ghana DPC registration](https://dataprotection.org.gh/registration/); [Act 843 text (NITA)](https://nita.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2017/12/Data-Protection-Act-2012-Act-843.pdf)
- **Sierra Leone:** as of the November 2025 announcement, the government "concluded the national validation" of the Data Protection and Right to Access Information Bill, 2025, with next steps being Cabinet approval then Parliament. A further April 2026 update shows Cabinet authorizing drafting/enactment of a unified Data Protection and Right to Access Information Act — i.e., **still pre-enactment** at this research date. [SierraLoaded — national validation](https://sierraloaded.sl/news/government-data-protection-information-bill/); [MoICE — validation conclusion](https://moice.gov.sl/moice-concludes-final-national-validation-of-the-data-protection-and-right-to-access-information-bill-2025/). The Cyber Security and Crime Act, 2021 is in force and covers cybercrime offenses, but sources consulted found no clearly detailed data-protection/breach-notification regime within it comparable to a dedicated DPA — commentary explicitly frames it as needing "a dedicated Data Protection Act" to complement it. [Institute for Legal Research and Advocacy for Justice](https://www.ilraj.org/publications/sierra-leones-cybersecurity-odyssey-progress-challenges-and-future-paths/); [full Act text, Sierra Leone Parliament](https://www.parliament.gov.sl/uploads/acts/THE%20CYBERSECURITY%20AND%20CRIME%20ACT,%202021%20-%20%2025TH%20NOVEMBER,%202021.pdf)
- **Critical issue (per build brief):** the merchant's customers are data subjects whose names, phone-adjacent identifiers, debt amounts, and payment behavior get stored, and they never consented to TradePal processing their data — only the merchant did. Lawful basis for processing a *non-user* third party's data must rest on **legitimate interest of the merchant's own bookkeeping**, narrowly scoped, not consent (consent from someone who has no relationship with TradePal is not obtainable in-product). This narrows what TradePal can store about a merchant's customer.

**Consequence for the build — data minimization/retention/deletion rules:**
- Do **not** store the customer's own WhatsApp number, only whatever locally-meaningful identifier the merchant uses (a name, a nickname, "John the tailor") — never resolve or store a phone number for someone who has not themselves interacted with TradePal.
- Store only what the ledger needs: name-as-given, running debt balance, transaction history, no biometric/behavioral profiling beyond the payer-risk label already specified (which must be derived purely from payment history, not AI inference, per the spec).
- Retention: financial records are append-only (per Non-Negotiable Standard #2) and retained per the merchant's own bookkeeping need — but a **deletion-request flow must exist for the merchant's customer** (e.g., a customer who is named in the merchant's debt book contacts TradePal to request their name/data be removed). Because the append-only ledger cannot delete posted transactions, the compliant mechanism is **anonymization of the customer-identifying label** (replace name with a generic placeholder) rather than deleting the financial record itself — this preserves ledger integrity while honoring the deletion right.
- Sierra Leone: apply the strictest of the NG/KE/GH baseline (effectively Kenya's or Nigeria's minimization+retention+rights standard) until Sierra Leone's own law is enacted, per the build brief's instruction and given the law is not yet in force.
- This requires new tables/flows already present in the data model list (`consent_logs`, `deletion_requests`) — Phase 1 must implement anonymization-on-deletion-request as the concrete mechanism, not a stub.

### KQ6 — Identity model

**Finding:** WhatsApp number as merchant identity is workable but needs explicit, product-defined behavior for the failure modes below — this is a product/support design question more than a technical one, and must be decided before Phase 1 onboarding flow is built.

**Recommended behavior (for approval, not yet built):**
- **SIM swap / number change:** merchant's business data is keyed to `businessId`, not phone number directly — `merchants.phone_number` is a mutable attribute of a merchant record. Support a merchant-initiated "change my number" flow gated by a challenge (e.g., a one-time code sent to the *old* number if still reachable, or a manual support/KYC-lite process if not) rather than allowing silent reassignment, since silent reassignment is the single biggest account-takeover risk in a phone-number-as-identity system.
- **Shared phone (two people running one shop):** treat as a single merchant identity — TradePal cannot distinguish speakers on WhatsApp. This is a known, accepted limitation to state plainly to users, not solved technically in MVP (no speaker diarization on text; even on voice, ASR speaker ID is out of scope). Document this as a stated limitation, not a silent gap.
- **Lost phone:** identity is tied to the WhatsApp number/account, not the device — if the merchant gets a new device but keeps their number and reinstalls WhatsApp, nothing changes. If they lose the number too, this collapses into the "number change" flow above with a higher-friction recovery challenge given higher fraud risk (someone else could claim to be recovering a merchant's account).
- **Account recovery without email:** since there is no email, recovery must be phone-number-based with a secondary verification factor — realistically a short human-support-assisted process for Phase 1 (manual verification against known business details: business name, recent transaction facts only the real merchant would know), not a fully automated self-serve flow. Full automation of this is a Phase 3+/4 problem, not Phase 1.

This did not require external research to resolve (a product decision, not a regulatory or platform fact) — flagging for explicit human sign-off since it affects the onboarding and recovery conversation design in Phase 1.

### KQ7 — Timezone and "today"

**Finding:** Standard, well-understood pattern — store all timestamps in UTC (`timestamptz`), store merchant timezone as a column on `businesses` (e.g., `Africa/Lagos`, `Africa/Nairobi`, `Africa/Accra`/`Africa/Dar_es_Salaam`, `Africa/Freetown`), and compute "today"/"this week" boundaries at query time by converting UTC to the merchant's local timezone before bucketing. This is already reflected correctly in the data model requirements ("All timestamps UTC... merchant timezone on businesses") — no change needed, just confirming it as correct and flagging it as a Phase 1 test requirement (a `/today` query run at 1am Lagos time must not include the previous UTC day's transactions incorrectly, and vice versa).

## 0.2 Architecture Decision Records

### ADR-1: WhatsApp access path — Direct Meta Cloud API (not a BSP)
- **Context:** Need reliable, cost-effective WhatsApp access across NG/KE/SL/GH with room to scale.
- **Decision:** Integrate directly against Meta's Cloud API rather than through a BSP (Twilio, 360dialog).
- **Consequences:** Lower long-run per-message cost (no BSP markup on top of Meta's own rate), but TradePal owns webhook infrastructure, signature verification, and template submission/approval directly — more upfront engineering than a BSP's managed dashboard would require. Business verification must be done once per legal entity, and Sierra Leone-market pricing is not yet distinctly priced by Meta (grouped under "Rest of Africa"), so Sierra Leone unit costs should be re-verified against Meta's live rate card at commercial launch, not assumed from research-date figures.

### ADR-2: LLM provider and model tier — cheap-tier default, capability tier as override
- **Context:** Need very low per-message cost given the target user base's price sensitivity, but also need reliable structured extraction from messy multilingual input.
- **Decision:** Default parsing model should be a low-cost tier (Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite or GPT-4o-mini class, ~$0.0001/message), with the ability to route specific low-confidence or multilingual-edge cases to a stronger model (Claude Haiku 4.5 class) behind a feature flag/config, not a hardcoded single model.
- **Consequences:** Keeps baseline unit economics extremely low (confirmed in KQ3); requires a provider-abstraction layer in the AI parsing service so the model can be swapped/tiered without rewriting the parsing pipeline; needs real evaluation (Phase 1 test suite) of whether the cheap tier's extraction quality is good enough on Nigerian Pidgin/Sheng/Krio/Twi input before committing exclusively to it.

### ADR-3: ASR provider — per-country config, no single vendor, Krio/Sierra Leone deferred
- **Context:** No ASR vendor covers Nigerian Pidgin, Sheng, Krio, and Twi adequately (KQ4).
- **Decision:** ASR provider is a per-country config value (e.g., general-purpose vendor for Kenya/standard Swahili; Ghana NLP Khaya AI or general vendor for Ghana/Twi with lowered confidence expectations; general vendor with explicit low-confidence-expected messaging for Nigeria/Pidgin). **Voice notes are disabled by feature flag for Sierra Leone at MVP launch** given no confirmed Krio ASR support exists.
- **Consequences:** More integration surface (multiple ASR vendors/config paths instead of one), but avoids shipping a broken/misleading voice feature in the market where it would do the most trust damage (Sierra Leone, where the brief already flags trust as the core product risk). Fallback-to-text-request must be implemented for every language, not just Krio, given low confidence expected across the board.

### ADR-4: Hosting region / data residency
- **Context:** Merchant and (non-consenting) customer personal and financial data is processed for users physically in West/East Africa; Nigeria's NDPA and Kenya's DPA both have cross-border transfer provisions requiring attention if data is hosted outside the country/region.
- **Decision (needs confirmation, flagged for approval):** Host in a region with strong operational maturity and, where possible, adequacy/safeguard mechanisms recognized by NDPC/ODPC — this needs a dedicated legal-transfer-mechanism review before Phase 1 database provisioning, not a default "just pick us-east" choice. This ADR is intentionally left open pending that review rather than guessed.
- **Consequences:** Hosting choice affects NDPA/DPA cross-border transfer compliance obligations (e.g., adequacy decisions, standard contractual clauses, or NDPC/ODPC-specific transfer mechanisms) — do not provision production infrastructure until this is resolved with actual legal review, since it is a compliance gate, not a performance/cost decision.

### ADR-5: Queue technology — Redis + BullMQ
- **Context:** Webhook handler must return 200 immediately and never do LLM calls inline (per platform rules); background jobs (reminders, reconciliation, billing) need idempotency-key support and safe re-run semantics.
- **Decision:** Redis-backed BullMQ, as already specified in the build brief's engineering standards.
- **Consequences:** Well-trodden Node/TypeScript-ecosystem choice with mature idempotency-key and retry support; no new research finding changes this — confirming it as sound rather than re-litigating it.

## Phase 1 — Build Report & Adversarial Self-Check

Scope built: Prisma schema/migrations, tenant-isolation extension, webhook handler (signature verification, dedupe, BullMQ enqueue), outbound WhatsApp gateway (merchant-only send path), country config, append-only ledger + reversal, AI parsing pipeline (confidence tiering), debt book + `/debt /paid /customer /today /undo /help /remind`, onboarding with consent logging, seed data, and the message dispatcher + BullMQ worker that wires all of the above into the actual queue consumer. **101 tests across 23 files pass** (`npx vitest run`), against a real Postgres (PGlite) test database via `tests/helpers/db.ts`, not mocks — `npx tsc --noEmit` is clean. (Original build: 82 tests/18 files; the four gap-closure modules below added 19 tests/5 files.)

### Where each Phase 0 decision landed in code

- **KQ1 (direct Cloud API, no BSP):** `src/whatsapp/webhookRoute.ts` verifies Meta's `X-Hub-Signature-256` directly (`tests/webhookSignature.test.ts`); no BSP SDK dependency exists in `package.json`.
- **KQ2 (no direct customer messaging):** enforced structurally, not just by convention — `sendWhatsAppTextMessage` (`src/whatsapp/outboundGateway.ts`) throws `OutboundSendRefusedError` for any `toPhoneNumber` that isn't a registered `Merchant.phoneNumber`, and `messageDispatcher.ts` routes every reply through this single function (`tests/outboundGateway.test.ts` covers the refusal). **Gap closed:** `/remind <name>` is now implemented (`src/domain/reminders.ts` + `commandRouter.ts`'s `handleRemind`), gated behind a `reminders` FeatureFlag (off by default, per Standard #7) — it never sends anything to the customer itself; it returns forwardable text and instructs the merchant to relay it, preserving the single-outbound-path guarantee. Persists a `Reminder` row per call. See `tests/reminders.test.ts` and the `/remind`-specific cases in `tests/commandRouter.test.ts`.
- **KQ3 (unit economics / provider abstraction):** `src/ai/provider.ts` defines `AiProvider` as an interface with `AnthropicAiProvider` as the concrete implementation, so the cheap-tier-vs-capability-tier routing decision from ADR-2 can be made later without touching `src/ai/parse.ts`. No cost telemetry is wired up yet (that needs real production volume, not a Phase 1 concern).
- **KQ4 (ASR/voice deferred):** Phase 1 implements **no voice/ASR path at all** — every inbound message with `message.text` absent (audio, image, etc.) gets `VOICE_NOT_SUPPORTED_REPLY` (`messageDispatcher.ts`, covered in `tests/messageDispatcher.test.ts`'s three-state voice test: pre-onboarding, mid-onboarding, fully onboarded). This is stricter than the KQ4 finding required (which only mandated deferring Sierra Leone/Krio) — text-only for *all four* countries at this stage is a deliberate, conservative simplification since no per-country ASR vendor selection exists yet. Correct direction, narrower scope than ADR-3 ultimately calls for.

### Phase 2 gap closure (this pass)

Three of the five originally-disclosed gaps have since been closed per explicit instruction ("tackle phase 2 gaps"); the other two (no frontend/UI, manual-only provisioning) are left as-is above, since one is out of scope for this pass and the other is a deliberate design constraint, not a defect.

## Phase 3 — Mobile Money Reconciliation — Build Report & Adversarial Self-Check

Scope built: merchant-facing mobile money reconciliation, the first Phase-3-marked capability called out directly in `prisma/schema.prisma` ("Mobile money reconciliation (Phase 3, schema now)" — the `MobileMoneyAlert` model and `MatchStatus` enum already existed from the Phase 0/1 schema pass, unused until now). The flow: a merchant forwards a provider's payment-confirmation SMS verbatim into WhatsApp via `/momo <text>`; TradePal parses it, records it, and tries to auto-suggest a matching `PAYMENT_RECEIVED` transaction already on the ledger; the merchant then confirms or rejects the suggestion. Billing (the schema's other Phase-3 marker) was deliberately **not** picked up in this pass — it's a separate, larger piece of scope and is left for a future phase, not silently dropped. **150 tests across 27 files pass** (`npx vitest run`, up from 126/26), `npx tsc --noEmit` is clean, and `npm run build` succeeds. No new migration was required — `MobileMoneyAlert` was already part of the initial schema migration.

### Where each piece landed in code

- **Domain layer:** `src/domain/mobileMoneyReconciliation.ts` — `parseMobileMoneyAlertText(rawText, minorUnitExp)` (two narrow, explicit regexes: an M-Pesa-style "Confirmed. You have received Ksh..." pattern and a generic "You have received XXX ... from ... Trans ID: ..." pattern; returns `null` — never a best-effort guess — for anything else, per Phase 0 KQ4's "never guess" posture), `recordMobileMoneyAlert` (idempotent on `providerTransactionId`, so forwarding the same SMS twice doesn't duplicate), `suggestMatchForAlert` (only auto-suggests when exactly one unambiguous `PAYMENT_RECEIVED` candidate exists — same amount, same currency, within a 48h window, not already linked to another alert; zero or multiple candidates leave it `UNMATCHED` rather than guessing), `confirmMobileMoneyMatch`/`rejectMobileMoneyMatch` (guarded by `MobileMoneyAlertNotFoundError`/`MobileMoneyAlertNotSuggestedError`, each recording an `AuditLog` row per Standard #8), and `listSuggestedMobileMoneyAlerts`. Phone numbers embedded in parsed SMS text are masked to their last 4 digits before being stored (`senderMasked`), in the same privacy-conscious spirit as KQ5's customer-data handling, even though `MobileMoneyAlert` isn't itself customer-identity data.
- **Commands:** `src/commands/commandRouter.ts` — `/momo <forwarded text>` (parses+records+auto-matches, or with no argument lists alerts awaiting confirmation), `/confirmmomo <id>`, `/rejectmomo <id>` (full alert UUID, not a short code — chosen for correctness/no-collision-risk when reconciling money, consistent with this project's general rigor around financial identifiers). All three are gated behind a new `mobileMoneyReconciliation` FeatureFlag (Standard #7, off by default), mirroring the existing `/remind` pattern exactly. `HELP_TEXT` and `prisma/seed.ts`'s flag-seeding were updated to match.
- **Admin surface:** `GET /admin/mobile-money-alerts` in `src/admin/adminRoutes.ts` — read-only, paginated (reuses `src/admin/pagination.ts`), optional `?businessId=` and `?status=` filters. `amountMinor` is explicitly serialized to a string before `res.json` (the first admin route to return a `bigint` column — Express's JSON serializer can't handle `BigInt` natively). Deliberately **no** admin-side confirm/reject endpoint was added in this pass — reconciliation is a merchant decision (they're the one who can recognize whether an SMS really matches a sale), so the write side stays WhatsApp-only; admin gets visibility, not a second write path to keep in sync.
- **Tests:** `tests/mobileMoneyReconciliation.test.ts` (15 tests — parser happy/unhappy paths including the two supported formats and two "never guess" fallback cases, dedupe-on-`providerTransactionId`, unique/zero/ambiguous-candidate matching behavior, confirm/reject state transitions + audit log rows, not-found/not-suggested error guards, and the pending-alerts list), `tests/commandRouter.test.ts` additions (feature-flag-off refusal, unrecognized-text guidance, auto-suggest → `/confirmmomo` end-to-end, `/rejectmomo` reverting to unmatched, not-found surfaced as a reply rather than a thrown error, and `/momo` with no arguments listing pending alerts), and `tests/adminRoutes.test.ts` additions (list + status filter + pagination shape, 401 when unauthenticated).

### Honest gaps — not built, disclosed rather than hidden

- **Narrow SMS format coverage.** Only two patterns are recognized (an M-Pesa-style format and one generic "You have received..." format loosely modeled on MTN/Airtel MoMo alerts). Real provider SMS formats vary by country, provider, and even change over time; anything that doesn't match falls through to "log it manually with `/paid` or `/debt`" rather than being guessed at — correct per KQ4, but it means real-world coverage is narrower than the four target countries' actual provider mix (e.g. Orange Money/Africell Money in Sierra Leone have no pattern here at all yet). Expanding coverage is expected, incremental follow-up work, not a one-time fix.
- **No automated ingestion.** The merchant must manually copy-paste/forward the SMS text into WhatsApp themselves; there is no SMS-forwarding integration (e.g. an Android forwarding app, a provider webhook/API) that captures alerts without merchant action. This phase is "make forwarded alerts useful," not "eliminate the forwarding step."
- **No fuzzy/tolerant matching.** Matching requires an *exact* amount and currency match within the time window. A common real-world case — the received amount differs slightly from the recorded sale because the provider deducted a fee — is not handled; such an alert will sit `UNMATCHED` and require the merchant to reconcile it manually. Deliberate for now (approximate matching risks silently pairing the wrong transaction, which is worse than requiring a manual look), but worth hardening later, e.g. by surfacing near-miss candidates to the merchant instead of hiding them entirely.
- **The `mobileMoneyReconciliation` flag is off by default everywhere** — as intended (Standard #7), but it means no business will see `/momo` until it's explicitly turned on per-business or the global default is flipped; there is no rollout tooling built yet for doing that at scale beyond direct `BusinessFeatureFlag` writes.

The full suite now passes **150 tests across 27 files**, `npx tsc --noEmit` remains clean, and `npm run build` still succeeds.

## Phase 6 — Automated Subscription-Expiry Enforcement — Build Report & Adversarial Self-Check

Scope built per explicit instruction ("go to the next phase," following Phase 5's report). Of the two legitimate candidates named at the end of Phase 5 — subscription-expiry enforcement, or further SMS-format coverage — this phase builds the former: a lapsed `Subscription.currentPeriodEnd` no longer leaves a business treated as fully entitled forever, closing the exact gap disclosed verbatim in both Phase 4 ("nothing in this codebase transitions a Subscription out of ACTIVE when its period lapses") and Phase 5. ### Where each piece landed in code

- **Read-time fix (the part that matters immediately): `getEffectivePlan` (`src/domain/billing.ts`)** now filters for `currentPeriodEnd: { gt: now }` on top of the existing `status: "ACTIVE"` filter, with `now` an optional parameter (default `new Date()`) threaded through from `getQuotaStatus`. ## Phase 7 — Subscription-Lapse WhatsApp Notification — Build Report & Adversarial Self-Check

Scope built per explicit instruction ("go ahead with your next legitimate flag," referring to the higher-value of the two candidates named at the end of Phase 6's report). Closes the gap disclosed in Phase 6: a business whose subscription lapses to `PAST_DUE` previously found out only by noticing quota enforcement kick in or by an admin checking the dashboard — nobody was told. This phase sends the business's own registered merchant(s) a WhatsApp message when the hourly sweep (or the manual admin trigger) lapses their subscription.

**Compliance check performed before writing any code:** `PHASE_0_FINDINGS.md`'s KQ2 finding (Meta policy forbids messaging a merchant's *customer* without that customer's own opt-in) does not apply here — this feature sends to the business's own `Merchant.phoneNumber`, about that business's own subscription, which is exactly the case Non-Negotiable Standard #9 and `sendWhatsAppTextMessage` already permit (the gateway is architecturally incapable of sending to a non-merchant number regardless). A separate, real risk was identified instead and is disclosed below rather than papered over: this is a business-initiated message that may fall outside the merchant's 24-hour WhatsApp service window, and this codebase's only outbound send path is free-form `type: "text"`, not a Meta-approved template — there is no proactive-outbound precedent anywhere else in this codebase to check against (the only existing outbound sends are reactive replies inside an open conversation window).

### Where each piece landed in code

- **New off-by-default `FeatureFlag`: `subscriptionLapseNotification`** (`prisma/seed.ts`), per Non-Negotiable Standard #7 — rolls out per-business via `BusinessFeatureFlag`, exactly like `reminders`/`mobileMoneyReconciliation`/`billingQuotaEnforcement` before it.
- **`src/domain/subscriptionExpiry.ts`:** `expireLapsedSubscriptions` now takes an optional third parameter, `outboundGateway?: Omit<OutboundGatewayDeps, "prisma">`. After each subscription's status/audit transition (unchanged from Phase 6), it checks the new flag for that business; if enabled **and** a gateway was supplied, it looks up every `Merchant` row for the business and sends each one a message via the existing `sendWhatsAppTextMessage` (the same single send path everything else in this codebase uses — no new gateway was built). Each send is wrapped in its own `try/catch`: a success records a new `AuditLog` row (`action: "SUBSCRIPTION_LAPSE_NOTIFICATION_SENT"`, `entityType: "Merchant"`), a failure records `"SUBSCRIPTION_LAPSE_NOTIFICATION_FAILED"` with the error message in `metadata` — one row per merchant per attempt, kept separate from the `SUBSCRIPTION_EXPIRED` row since the send is a distinct, independently-failable effect (Standard #8). A failed send for one merchant/business never aborts the sweep for anyone else. When the flag is on but no gateway was supplied (e.g., a process that doesn't have WhatsApp credentials configured), the function fails safe — it skips sending rather than throwing.
- **`src/subscriptionExpiryWorker.ts`** (the hourly cron consumer) now reads `WHATSAPP_ACCESS_TOKEN`/`WHATSAPP_PHONE_NUMBER_ID` via the same `requireEnv` pattern `src/worker.ts` already uses, and passes them through to every sweep call — this is the primary path notifications actually go out on.
- **`src/server.ts`** reads the same two env vars, but optionally (not `requireEnv`) — the API server process doesn't otherwise need WhatsApp send credentials to boot, so this doesn't force every deployment to configure them just for the API to start. When present, they're threaded through `createAdminRouter`'s new `outboundGateway` option so the manual `POST /admin/maintenance/expire-subscriptions` trigger can also notify; when absent, that route still runs the sweep, just without notifications.
- **Message content:** a fixed, non-templated string naming the lapsed plan code and directing the merchant to contact support to renew — kept deliberately generic (no business name lookup added) since the subscription row only carries a `businessId`, not the business's display name, and adding that join wasn't necessary for the message to be useful.
- **Tests:** 4 new tests appended to `tests/subscriptionExpiry.test.ts`: flag off + gateway supplied sends nothing; flag on + no gateway supplied sends nothing (and doesn't throw); flag on + gateway supplied sends one message per registered merchant (multi-merchant business, OWNER + STAFF) and records one `SENT` audit row each with the right `entityId`/`metadata`; a non-2xx Meta API response is caught, recorded as a `FAILED` audit row with the error text, and still lets `expireLapsedSubscriptions` return `expiredCount: 1` rather than propagating the send failure as a thrown error.

### Honest gaps — not built, disclosed rather than hidden

- **Free-form text, not a Meta-approved template.** As flagged above: if a merchant hasn't messaged the bot within the preceding 24 hours, Meta's WhatsApp Cloud API can reject this send outright, since business-initiated messages outside the service window are supposed to use an approved template. This codebase has no template-message infrastructure anywhere (this is the first proactive/system-initiated send it has ever attempted), and building one was out of scope for this pass. Failures are now at least observable (the new `FAILED` audit rows), which they were not before this phase, but the underlying delivery risk is not eliminated.
- **No retry.** A failed send (rate limit, transient network error, expired access token) is recorded and then dropped — the next hourly sweep tick won't re-attempt it, since the subscription is already `PAST_DUE` by then and no longer matches the lapsed-`ACTIVE` query. A merchant whose notification failed to send has no automatic second chance; an admin would have to notice the `FAILED` audit row and act manually (e.g. via the existing admin audit-log view).
- **No admin-frontend surface for this flag.** Turning `subscriptionLapseNotification` on for a business today requires a direct `setFeatureFlagForBusiness` call or database write — there is no admin-frontend UI toggle, unlike some other admin-manageable settings. Consistent with how every other `FeatureFlag` in this codebase is rolled out (none have a frontend toggle yet), not a new gap unique to this feature.
- **Message is not localized.** Every other merchant-facing string in this codebase is generated per the business's `languageCode` (see the `/remind`, ledger-confirmation, and onboarding flows) — this notification is a single hardcoded English string regardless of the business's configured language. A real inconsistency with the rest of the product, disclosed rather than fixed silently, since localizing it correctly means finding and reusing this codebase's existing i18n string-lookup mechanism rather than guessing at one.
- **Still no payment collection** — unchanged from every prior phase; the notification tells a merchant their subscription lapsed and to contact support, but there remains no in-product way for them to renew it themselves.

The full backend suite now passes **207 tests across 30 files** (`npx vitest run`, up from 203/30), `npx tsc --noEmit` is clean, and `npm run build` succeeds.

**Template-message support** (closes Phase 7's "free-form text, not a Meta-approved template" gap):

- **`src/whatsapp/outboundGateway.ts`:** refactored the existing `sendWhatsAppTextMessage` to share two new helpers — `assertRegisteredMerchant` (the Standard #9 registered-merchant guard) and `postToGraphApi` (the actual `fetch` call plus non-2xx error handling) — and added `sendWhatsAppTemplateMessage(deps, message)`, sending a `type: "template"` payload (`template: { name, language: { code }, components? }`) instead of `type: "text"`. `components` (an array with one `{ type: "body", parameters: [...] }` entry) is only included when `bodyParams` is non-empty, matching what Meta's Graph API expects. The refactor deliberately left `sendWhatsAppTextMessage`'s own request body and behavior unchanged — verified against its pre-existing test's exact expected JSON shape.
- **`src/domain/subscriptionExpiry.ts`:** `SubscriptionExpiryOutboundGateway` (the type `expireLapsedSubscriptions`'s third parameter now takes) gained an optional `lapseNotificationTemplate?: { name; languageCode }`. When present, the sweep calls `sendWhatsAppTemplateMessage` with the lapsed plan's code as the template's single body parameter, instead of `sendWhatsAppTextMessage`; when absent, behavior is byte-for-byte the same free-form-text send Phase 7 shipped. Both the `SENT` and `FAILED` audit-log rows now also record which `sendMethod` (`"text"` or `"template"`) was actually used, so this choice is auditable per-send, not just per-deployment.
- **`src/config/outboundGatewayEnv.ts`** (new file): `buildSubscriptionExpiryOutboundGatewayFromEnv()` centralizes reading `WHATSAPP_ACCESS_TOKEN`/`WHATSAPP_PHONE_NUMBER_ID` (required together for any gateway at all) and the two new, further-optional `WHATSAPP_SUBSCRIPTION_LAPSE_TEMPLATE_NAME`/`WHATSAPP_SUBSCRIPTION_LAPSE_TEMPLATE_LANGUAGE` vars, shared by `src/server.ts` and `src/subscriptionExpiryWorker.ts` so the two entrypoints can't silently drift on how they build this config (they previously duplicated similar env-reading logic inline).
- Using this requires Meta to have actually approved a template for this phone number first — TradePal has no way to create or verify one itself; leaving both template env vars unset keeps the exact Phase 7 free-form-text behavior, so this is a strict opt-in add, not a behavior change for anyone not using it.

**Retry-on-failure** (closes Phase 7's "no retry" gap):

- **`src/domain/subscriptionExpiry.ts`:** a new `sendLapseNotificationWithRetry(sendFn)` wraps each per-merchant send attempt in up to `NOTIFICATION_MAX_ATTEMPTS = 3` tries, waiting `NOTIFICATION_RETRY_DELAYS_MS = [250, 750]` between them. Not every failure is retried: `isRetryableNotificationError` inspects the thrown error and only retries on a Meta API 429 (rate limit) or 5xx (their-side transient failure) — or on an error whose status code it can't parse at all — never on `OutboundSendRefusedError` (the registered-merchant guard; retrying a structurally-refused send would never succeed) or any other 4xx (e.g. a malformed request, which retrying also can't fix). Both outcomes are now visible in the audit trail: a `SENT` row records how many attempts it took (1 if it succeeded first try), and a `FAILED` row (only recorded once all attempts are exhausted) records the total attempts made alongside the final error.
- This directly narrows, but doesn't eliminate, the gap Phase 7 disclosed: a merchant whose notification fails all 3 attempts within the same sweep tick still gets no further automatic retry (the subscription is already `PAST_DUE` by the next tick and no longer matches the lapsed-`ACTIVE` query, exactly as before) — see Honest Gaps below.

**Additional mobile-money SMS coverage** (continuing the incremental hardening named as an open candidate since Phase 5):

- **`src/domain/mobileMoneyReconciliation.ts`:** added `MPESA_TILL_ALERT_PATTERN`, a new, distinct M-Pesa shape for payments received into a Till/Paybill (business) account rather than a personal account. Safaricom's own confirmation text drops "You have" for this case — "`<ID> Confirmed. Ksh<amount> received from <name> <phone> ...`" vs. the pre-existing P2P shape's "`<ID> Confirmed. You have received Ksh<amount> from <name> <phone> ...`" — a real, well-known, documented difference between the two SMS shapes, not an invented one. This was prioritized as the single highest-value addition (over covering more brands/countries) because TradePal's actual users are retail merchants, who in Kenya overwhelmingly receive customer payments via a Till/Paybill number rather than a personal M-Pesa account — the pre-existing pattern set could not recognize the SMS shape merchants most commonly forward.
- A regression test confirms the new Till pattern and the pre-existing P2P pattern don't interfere with each other (each text is still routed to the correct one).
- This is deliberately a small, one-pattern addition, not an attempt at exhaustive coverage — consistent with every prior phase's framing of SMS coverage as ongoing, incremental hardening rather than a one-time completable task.

**Localization — investigated, not built:**

- Before writing any code, checked whether this codebase has an i18n/string-lookup mechanism to hook into (rather than inventing one). It does not: `languageCode` is stored as metadata on `Reminder` and `Business` rows, but every merchant-facing string actually generated anywhere in this codebase (`src/domain/reminders.ts`, `src/domain/subscriptionExpiry.ts`'s lapse notification, onboarding, ledger confirmations, etc.) is hardcoded English — `languageCode` is written but never read for this purpose. Confirmed further that all four `SUPPORTED_COUNTRIES` (`src/config/countries.ts`) currently default to `defaultLanguage: "en"`, and `prisma/seed.ts` only ever seeds a single `Language` row (`en`) — there is no second language anywhere in the running system today.
- Given that, "localizing" the Phase 7 lapse notification specifically would mean either (a) building a whole new translation-string-lookup mechanism from scratch and inventing its first translations myself, or (b) hardcoding a handful of guessed non-English strings with no existing pattern to follow and no native speaker/reviewer in the loop. Sierra Leone (Krio), Kenya (Sheng/Swahili), Ghana (Twi), and Nigeria (Pidgin) are all real, distinct languages/registers where a wrong or unnatural machine-guessed translation sent to a real merchant is a worse outcome than the current, honest, single-language English — and inventing the very first i18n architecture this codebase would ever have, unreviewed, for one notification string, is a disproportionate and premature scope for this pass.
- Disclosed as an honest non-build rather than shipped as invented, unvetted translations: the codebase-wide English-only gap (already implicitly present since Phase 1 for every other merchant-facing string) is unchanged by this phase, not newly introduced by it.

## Phase 10 — Feature-Flag Admin UI + Bulk Rollout — Build Report & Adversarial Self-Check

Scope built per explicit instruction ("yes go for it"), following a research-grounded recommendation (a codebase survey confirmed feature flags had no admin tooling at all — disclosed as a gap in Phases 3, 4, and 7 without ever being addressed) presented alongside the other legitimate remaining candidates, of which this was the clear top pick: it closes a three-times-disclosed gap, needs no new schema (the `FeatureFlag`/`BusinessFeatureFlag` tables have existed since Phase 1), and — unlike payment collection — requires no human gateway decision and no handling of financial credentials.

### Where each piece landed in code

**Domain layer (`src/domain/featureFlags.ts`):**

- `setFeatureFlagForBusiness` (pre-existing) now validates the flag actually exists first (throwing a new `FeatureFlagNotFoundError` instead of letting a bad key hit the database as a raw foreign-key error) and records one `AuditLog` row per call (`BUSINESS_FEATURE_FLAG_SET`) — this was previously completely unaudited, since every prior caller was test setup, not a real admin action.
- New `removeFeatureFlagOverrideForBusiness` reverts a business to the flag's current global default by deleting its override row outright (not by copying today's default into `enabled`) — so it stays correct even if the global default changes again later. Idempotent: returns `false` and records no audit row if there was nothing to remove.
- New `listFeatureFlags` (all flags, for the global admin list) and `setFeatureFlagGlobalDefault` (the actual bulk-rollout/rollback mechanism — flips a flag's `enabledByDefault`, immediately changing behavior for every business that has never set its own override). Mirroring `upsertPlan`'s precedent for global reference-data, this records no `AuditLog` row and takes a raw `PrismaClient`, not a `TenantScopedClient` — a global default change doesn't belong to any single business.
- New `listFeatureFlagStatesForBusiness` — the merged per-business view (`{ key, description, enabledByDefault, override, effective }` per flag) the business-detail admin page needs, computed in one pass rather than one round trip per flag.
- A deliberate design choice, documented in-code: the domain layer validates only that the flag exists, not that the business does — a business-existence check was pushed to the admin-route layer instead (mirroring `GET /admin/businesses/:id`'s own inline 404), because the obvious alternative (importing `BusinessNotFoundError` from `planAdmin.ts`) would create a circular ES-module import (`featureFlags.ts → planAdmin.ts → billing.ts → featureFlags.ts`, since `billing.ts` already imports `isFeatureEnabled` from `featureFlags.ts`). Caught and corrected at design time, before any code referencing it was ever run.

**Admin routes (`src/admin/adminRoutes.ts`), five new endpoints, following this codebase's established POST-only-for-writes convention:**

- `GET /admin/feature-flags` — open to any authenticated role (read-only), lists every flag.
- `POST /admin/feature-flags/:key` — `SUPER_ADMIN` only (stricter than the per-business routes below, since flipping a global default is a bulk rollout with a materially bigger blast radius); calls `setFeatureFlagGlobalDefault`, 404s on `FeatureFlagNotFoundError`.
- `GET /admin/businesses/:id/feature-flags` — any role; 404s if the business doesn't exist (the inline check this module's design intentionally delegates here); otherwise returns the merged per-business states.
- `POST /admin/businesses/:id/feature-flags/:key` — `SUPER_ADMIN`/`SUPPORT` (mirroring the existing phone-number-change and subscription-management precedent); sets a business's override, attributing the acting admin and an optional `reason`.
- `POST /admin/businesses/:id/feature-flags/:key/reset` — same RBAC; removes a business's override, reverting it to the global default.

**Admin frontend:**

- New `FeatureFlagsPage.tsx` (mirrors `PlansPage.tsx`'s structure) — a global list of every flag with a `SUPER_ADMIN`-only toggle button per row that flips `enabledByDefault` (the actual bulk-rollout control), added to the nav bar and router.
- A new "Feature flags" section on `BusinessDetailPage.tsx` (mirrors the existing `SubscriptionSection`/`MerchantRow` inline-form pattern) showing every flag's global default, this business's override (or "— (follows default)"), and the computed effective boolean, with `SUPER_ADMIN`/`SUPPORT`-only controls to force-enable, force-disable, or reset a business's override, with an optional audit-logged reason.

- `npx tsc --noEmit` — clean (backend).
- Full backend suite: **237/237 tests across 30 files** (up from 216/30) — 14 new tests in `tests/featureFlags.test.ts` (the now-audited `setFeatureFlagForBusiness`, `removeFeatureFlagOverrideForBusiness` including its idempotent no-op case, `listFeatureFlags`, `setFeatureFlagGlobalDefault` including the no-audit-row assertion and the override-survives-rollout case, and `listFeatureFlagStatesForBusiness`'s merged view), and a new 12-test "feature flag admin routes (Phase 10)" block in `tests/adminRoutes.test.ts` covering RBAC (ANALYST/SUPPORT forbidden where appropriate, SUPER_ADMIN/SUPPORT allowed where appropriate) and 404 handling for unknown businesses/flags on all five new routes.

## Phase 11 — Voice-Note Transcription (OpenAI Whisper) — Build Report & Adversarial Self-Check

Scope built per explicit instruction ("yes to all"), following a fuller back-and-forth: the user was first asked whether to close the previously-disclosed `Plan.voiceEnabled` dead-code gap ("do you want deal with 3"), replied "do all of them" (read as choosing to build real voice support rather than the alternative of honestly retiring the field, since building real support structurally resolves what retiring it would have addressed), was then asked which STT vendor to use, replied "not sure, may be leave it open. what is your suggestion," was given a researched recommendation (OpenAI Whisper, mirroring the existing `AiProvider` provider-abstraction pattern, no live API key needed in chat), and finally confirmed with "yes to all." Before writing any code, this codebase's own prior research was re-read rather than re-derived from memory: `PHASE_0_FINDINGS` already had an unimplemented ADR-3/KQ4 finding — no single ASR vendor covers all four launch languages, Sierra Leone/Krio has zero confirmed vendor support anywhere, and a low-confidence transcript must never be silently trusted — plus two already-existing, previously-unused schema fields built for exactly this (`CountryConfig.voiceEnabled`, pre-seeded `false` for Sierra Leone, and `Plan.voiceEnabled` itself). This phase wires both up rather than building a naive single-gate integration that would have silently reintroduced the Krio risk ADR-3 already flagged.

### Where each piece landed in code

- **`src/stt/provider.ts`** (new): `SttProvider` interface (`transcribe(request): Promise<string>`, empty string = "no intelligible speech") + `WhisperSttProvider`, mirroring `src/ai/provider.ts`'s `AiProvider`/`AnthropicAiProvider` split (ADR-2) so the dispatcher never calls the Whisper HTTP API directly and the vendor can be swapped later without touching `messageDispatcher.ts`. Uses `response_format: "json"` (just `{ text }`) rather than `verbose_json`'s per-segment confidence data — deliberately, since this codebase already has a proven "don't guess" safety net at the AI-parse layer (`parseTransactionText`'s confidence/`requiresClarification` handling), and re-deriving an ASR-specific confidence threshold from `no_speech_prob` here would be new, unvalidated guesswork rather than reuse of something already tested. Uses Node 24's global `FormData`/`Blob` for the multipart upload — no new dependency.
- **`src/whatsapp/mediaGateway.ts`** (new): `downloadWhatsAppMedia(deps, mediaId)` implements Meta's two-step Media API flow — `GET /{media-id}` (bearer-token-authenticated) resolves a short-lived download `url` + `mime_type`, then a second bearer-token-authenticated `GET` on that URL fetches the actual bytes. Falls back to `application/octet-stream` if Meta's metadata response omits `mime_type`.
- **`src/messageDispatcher.ts`:** `DispatcherDeps` gained an optional `sttProvider?: SttProvider | undefined` — omitting it (mirroring `OPENAI_API_KEY` being unset) means voice transcription is skipped entirely, not that dispatch crashes, following the exact optional-credential precedent Phase 8 set for `subscriptionExpiryWorker.ts`'s WhatsApp credentials. A new off-by-default `voiceTranscription` FeatureFlag (Standard #7) gates the feature. The core addition is `resolveVoiceNote`, which requires all three of the following before any network call is made: (1) the `voiceTranscription` flag is enabled for the business, (2) `CountryConfig.voiceEnabled` is true for the business's own country (false for Sierra Leone per ADR-3 — no wasted cost/time on a near-guaranteed-failing Krio transcription), and (3) `Plan.voiceEnabled` is true for the business's currently-effective plan (via the existing `getEffectivePlan`). Only once all three pass does it call `downloadWhatsAppMedia` then `sttProvider.transcribe`; a genuinely empty transcript or any thrown error (network failure, non-2xx from Whisper, etc.) is treated as `transcription_failed`, never silently trusted or passed downstream. `dispatchInboundMessage`'s fully-onboarded voice-message branch now tries `resolveVoiceNote` before falling back to the pre-existing "voice not supported" reply; the transcript, once obtained, is handed to the exact same `dispatchCommandOrParse` path typed text already goes through — so the AI-parse confidence/clarification step doubles as the noisy-transcript safety net, rather than this function inventing its own. The mid-onboarding voice-message branch is deliberately untouched — onboarding stays text-only, unconditionally, regardless of any gate. Per Standard #8, `resolveVoiceNote` deliberately records no `AuditLog` row of its own: transcribing a merchant's own voice note isn't an admin/business-state-changing action, and whatever it resolves to (a logged sale, a command, a clarification request) is already covered by that path's own logging (`AiParseLog`).
- **`src/worker.ts`:** reads `OPENAI_API_KEY` optionally (a `console.warn` and `sttProvider` left `undefined` if unset, not `requireEnv`) and constructs a `WhisperSttProvider` when present, added to the `deps` object passed to `dispatchInboundMessage`.
- **`prisma/seed.ts`:** new `voiceTranscription` FeatureFlag upsert, `enabledByDefault: false`, alongside the existing `subscriptionLapseNotification` entry.
- **`.env.example`:** documented the new optional `OPENAI_API_KEY`, with a comment explaining exactly which gates (FeatureFlag, `CountryConfig.voiceEnabled`, `Plan.voiceEnabled`) also have to pass before it does anything, and that leaving it unset means voice notes behave exactly as before this phase.

## Phase 12 — Weekly Business-Health Digest (Competitive Differentiation) — Build Report & Adversarial Self-Check

Scope built per explicit instruction ("what do you want to build next. continue with the next build. what features do you want to add in this that my competitor don't have."), a compound request combining an open invitation to exercise judgment, a direct instruction to proceed, and a strategic question this report answers directly rather than deferring.

**Competitive research (live WebSearch, not memory):** the closest bookkeeping-adjacent competitor is **Kippa** (Nigerian app-based sales/expense tracking, digital receipts, debt recovery, banking), with **Bumpa** (retail automation/e-commerce/inventory) similar — both require the merchant to open a dedicated app; neither lets a merchant log a sale by just typing/speaking it into a chat they already have open. **ModSapp, Woya, Relay (Ghana), SmartBizSystems, and Intelli (Kenya/multi-country)** are genuinely WhatsApp-native, but are **customer-facing order-taking/sales bots** — they help a merchant sell *to* their own customers over WhatsApp, a fundamentally different product from TradePal's merchant-facing, natural-language bookkeeping. No competitor found does **proactive, automated, WhatsApp-native financial-health nudges** — every one of them is pull-based (the merchant has to go open something to see their numbers). That gap is what this phase builds toward.

**Internal codebase research** also surfaced two more "designed-for-but-never-wired-up" schema fields, mirroring the exact shape `Plan.voiceEnabled` turned out to be before Phase 11: `Merchant.role: MerchantRole` (`OWNER`/`STAFF`, fully defined, completely unused in any domain logic) and `InventoryItem`/`InventoryMovement` (a full stock-tracking schema, unused — the `STOCK_ADJUSTMENT` intent is recognized by the AI parser but hard-blocked with a "not available yet" reply). ## Phase 13 — Multi-Staff Support (`/addstaff`) — Build Report & Adversarial Self-Check

Scope chosen per explicit instruction ("continue with the. next phase"), which — consistent with the standing pattern of deferring phase-selection judgment — required picking between Phase 12's two named candidates myself: stock/inventory tracking (wiring up the unused `InventoryItem`/`InventoryMovement` schema) and multi-staff support (wiring up the unused `Merchant.role`/`MerchantRole` enum). Multi-staff support was chosen as the more competitively differentiated of the two: it closes KQ6's shared-phone limitation (a shop with more than one person serving customers currently has to share a single WhatsApp number/device to use TradePal at all), a limitation every competitor surfaced in Phase 12's research (Kippa, Bumpa, and the WhatsApp-order-bots alike) shares just as much, since none of them are architected around a shared business identity spanning multiple individual phone numbers either.

Before writing any code, a dedicated research pass (reading `onboardingFlow.ts`, `messageDispatcher.ts`, `commandRouter.ts`'s `CommandContext`, the full `Merchant`/`MerchantRole`/`Business`/`ConsentLog` schema, and every existing test touching merchant identity) confirmed the schema already supports this with **zero migration**: `MerchantRole` (`OWNER`/`STAFF`) has existed since Phase 1 schema but was never read anywhere; `Business.merchants` is already a one-to-many relation with no unique constraint limiting one Merchant per Business; and outbound-broadcast code (`businessDigest.ts`, `subscriptionExpiry.ts`) already iterates `scoped.merchant.findMany({ where: { businessId } })`, proving multiple Merchant rows per business are already exercised, just never *created* by anything other than `startOnboarding`. The one real gap found: `CommandContext` (in `commandRouter.ts`) carried zero merchant identity at all — no `merchantId`, no `role` — so no command could ever have been role-gated even in principle.

### Where each piece landed in code

- **`src/domain/merchantIdentity.ts`:** added `addStaffMerchant(prisma, { businessId, phoneNumber, invitedByMerchantId })`, sitting alongside the existing `changeMerchantPhoneNumber`/`PhoneNumberAlreadyRegisteredError` and following the exact same pattern: the phone-number-collision check runs on the **unscoped** `prisma` client (since `Merchant.phoneNumber` is `@unique` globally, not per-business, a business-scoped client could only ever see a collision within its own tenant), while the actual `create` and its `AuditLog` row (`STAFF_MERCHANT_ADDED`) go through `getTenantScopedClient`. The key design decision: the new Merchant row is created with `onboardingStep: "AWAITING_CONSENT"`, **not** `"COMPLETE"` — deliberately not skipping consent capture even though it does skip Business-creation and the business-name question (this business already has both). This reuses an *existing* `OnboardingStep` enum value rather than inventing a new one (e.g. some `AWAITING_STAFF_CONSENT`), which is exactly why no schema migration was needed: the very next inbound WhatsApp message from the new phone number falls into `dispatchInboundMessage`'s existing "merchant found but `!isOnboardingComplete`" branch, which calls the existing `continueOnboarding` → `handleAwaitingConsent`, capturing the same two `ConsentLog` rows (`ONBOARDING_TERMS`, `DATA_PROCESSING`) a brand-new merchant gets. `onboardingFlow.ts` itself was not touched at all.
- **`src/commands/commandRouter.ts`:** `CommandContext` gained three fields: `prisma: PrismaClient` (the raw/unscoped client, documented as needed only for `/addstaff`'s cross-tenant uniqueness check — every other handler must keep using `scopedPrisma`), `merchantId: string`, and `merchantRole: MerchantRole`. New `handleAddStaff`, gated behind a new off-by-default `staffAccounts` FeatureFlag (Standard #7) and, once the flag is on, further gated to `ctx.merchantRole === "OWNER"` — a STAFF-role merchant is refused with a plain message, never a crash. Input is normalized by stripping all non-digit characters (`args.trim().replace(/\D/g, "")`), so an owner typing `/addstaff +234 805 555 0001` still resolves to the same digits-only convention `onboardingFlow.ts` already uses for `phoneNumber`. `/addstaff` was added to `HELP_TEXT` and the command switch.
- **`src/messageDispatcher.ts`:** `dispatchCommandOrParse`'s `CommandContext` construction now threads `prisma: deps.prisma`, `merchantId: merchant.id`, and `merchantRole: merchant.role` — all three were already available as the existing `merchant` parameter and `deps.prisma`, so this required no new lookups, just wiring already-in-scope data through.
- **`prisma/seed.ts`:** new `staffAccounts` FeatureFlag upsert, `enabledByDefault: false`, alongside the existing flags.

### Honest gaps — not built, disclosed rather than hidden

- **The new staff member is not proactively notified.** `/addstaff` only replies to the *owner* (the one who sent the command); the new phone number receives nothing until it messages the bot for the first time. This was a deliberate scope decision: proactively messaging an arbitrary new number from inside `commandRouter.ts` would require threading outbound-send capability into a module that has always been pure text-in/text-out (every other outbound send in this codebase happens centrally in `messageDispatcher.ts`'s `reply()`), a larger change than this phase's actual gap justified. The practical impact is that the owner must separately tell the new staff member (verbally, or by forwarding a message) to text the bot — a real, live UX gap, not a hidden one.
- **No cap on staff count per business.** Any number of STAFF merchants can be added; there is no `Plan`-tier limit (unlike `entryCapPerMonth`) and no admin visibility into how many staff a business has beyond querying the database directly. A future phase could add a `Plan.maxStaffCount` field and enforce it in `handleAddStaff`, mirroring `assertWithinQuotaIfEnabled`'s existing pattern.
- **No `/removestaff` (or any way to revoke a staff member's access) exists yet.** This phase only builds the "add" half; removing a STAFF merchant (or demoting/promoting role) currently requires direct database access, the same gap Phase 4 originally disclosed for plan/subscription management before Phase 5 closed it with admin tooling.
- **Every command a STAFF merchant can run is currently identical to what an OWNER can run, except `/addstaff` itself.** No other command is role-gated (e.g. a STAFF member can `/undo` a transaction, or theoretically issue `/addstaff` if the flag/role check is ever loosened). This is a deliberate minimal-scope choice — `/addstaff` was the one command that clearly needed gating — not an oversight, but a business relying on staff accounts to *restrict* what staff can do (rather than simply extend who can log entries) would need finer-grained permissions this phase doesn't build.

- **No live-WhatsApp-account verification** — every test drives `dispatchInboundMessage`/`handleCommand` directly or mocks `fetch`, matching every prior phase's own disclosed limitation.
- **Stock/inventory tracking, payment collection, and richer digest content all remain unbuilt** — unchanged from Phase 12's own disclosed list; stock/inventory tracking in particular remains the other named Phase-12 candidate not chosen this round.

### Stock/inventory tracking

- **`src/domain/inventory.ts` (new):** `recordStockAdjustment`, `getInventoryStatus`, and `setLowStockThreshold`. ### Phase 13 gap closures

- **Digest idempotency marker's crash-window double-send risk (fixed):** `businessDigestWorker.ts`'s `WEEKLY_DIGEST_PERIOD_PROCESSED` `AuditLog` marker is now written **before** the per-merchant send loop runs, not after. This converts a "process crashes mid-send-loop → marker never written → next sweep re-sends to every merchant already sent" failure mode into an "process crashes mid-send-loop → some merchants for this business never get sent this week" failure mode — judged the safer trade-off for a notification feature (an under-send is a missed nudge; a double-send is a business getting the same financial summary twice and wondering if something's wrong).
- **Proactive staff-added notification (built):** `addStaffMerchant` now optionally accepts an `outboundGateway` and, once the new STAFF row is committed (so Standard #9's registered-merchant send guard passes), sends it a WhatsApp message telling it to message the bot to finish setup — closing the gap where an owner had to manually forward the news. Reuses the shared `sendWithRetry` module (see below) via its own `StaffNotificationSendFailedError` wrapper. The send is fully best-effort: success is audited as `STAFF_MERCHANT_ADD_NOTIFICATION_SENT`, failure as `STAFF_MERCHANT_ADD_NOTIFICATION_FAILED`, but a send failure never throws out of `addStaffMerchant` — the staff row itself is already committed by the time the notification is attempted, so a WhatsApp outage can never turn a successful `/addstaff` into a reported failure.
- **Staff-count cap tied to `Plan` (built):** `Plan.staffCapCount: Int?` (null = uncapped, mirroring `entryCapPerMonth`'s existing convention), threaded through `getEffectivePlan`'s returned `EffectivePlan`, and enforced in `addStaffMerchant` by counting existing active (`removedAt: null`) STAFF rows — the OWNER row is never counted, and a removed STAFF row no longer holds a slot. Checked after the phone-collision check (a doomed request should fail for the more specific reason first) but before creating the row, so a request that would exceed the cap never touches the database. Threaded through the admin API (`upsertPlanBodySchema`'s `staffCapCount`, optional so an old admin-frontend client can't silently clobber a manually-set cap — omitted means "don't touch," explicit `null` means "clear it").
- **`/removestaff` (built):** the counterpart to `/addstaff`. Soft-deactivates a STAFF row via a new `Merchant.removedAt: DateTime?` field (schema addition) rather than deleting it, preserving `AuditLog`/`ConsentLog` history. The OWNER can never be removed this way. `messageDispatcher.ts`'s `dispatchInboundMessage` now gates on `removedAt` — a removed number's inbound traffic gets a fixed "no longer has access" reply and nothing else runs (parsing, commands, onboarding), though the branch still falls through to mark the webhook `PROCESSED`, consistent with every other branch. Deliberately does **not** support reactivating a previously-removed number back to STAFF — that needs a real decision about whether fresh consent is required on return, left as a disclosed gap below.
- **Fine-grained per-command role gating (documented, not built):** a doc comment on `handleRemoveStaff` now explicitly states why role-gating stops at the two roster-management commands (`/addstaff`, `/removestaff`) rather than extending to every ordinary operational command — this was already disclosed as a gap in Phase 13 and remains a deliberate minimal-scope choice, not an oversight, so this phase records the reasoning in code rather than silently re-disclosing the same gap a second time.

Staff reactivation.** Phase 14 disclosed that a removed staff number (`Merchant.removedAt` set) had no path back in short of a human/support-assisted change. `addStaffMerchant` in `src/domain/merchantIdentity.ts` now distinguishes a same-business, still-removed collision from every other kind: instead of unconditionally throwing `PhoneNumberAlreadyRegisteredError`, it clears `removedAt`, resets `onboardingStep` back to `AWAITING_CONSENT` (so the number re-walks the exact same consent flow a brand-new staff row would), and re-applies the existing staff-cap check — cross-business collisions and same-business-but-still-active collisions are unaffected and still refuse. ### Implementation

**1. **2. The schema gained a new `PENDING` `SubscriptionStatus` value (a checkout link was generated but payment isn't yet confirmed — distinct from `ACTIVE` so an incomplete checkout doesn't look subscribed, and from `PAST_DUE` so the lapse sweep, which only ever targets `ACTIVE` rows, never touches it) and two new `Invoice` fields, `providerCode`/`providerReference` (the latter `@unique`), reconciling a Flutterwave webhook or Verify Transaction response back to the `Invoice` that requested it via a TradePal-generated `tx_ref` — generated before the checkout link even exists, so it can be stored on the `Invoice` row up front, and echoed back unchanged by Flutterwave on both the webhook payload and the verify call. A new `src/flutterwave/` package (`client.ts`, `signature.ts`) wraps Flutterwave's `POST /v3/payments` (create a hosted checkout link) and `GET /v3/transactions/:id/verify` (server-to-server confirmation) calls, plus the webhook's shared-secret `verif-hash` header check via `crypto.timingSafeEqual` — a static string comparison, not an HMAC-over-payload like WhatsApp's own webhook signature, per Flutterwave's own documented webhook design; this is also why, per Flutterwave's own integration guidance, the webhook payload is never trusted directly and every webhook triggers a Verify Transaction call before anything is considered confirmed. The new `src/domain/payments.ts` (`initiateSubscriptionCheckout`, `confirmSubscriptionPayment`) is off by default behind a new `paymentCollection` FeatureFlag (Standard #7) — this codebase's first feature that moves real money, so the off-by-default bar applies more, not less. On confirmation it grants a fixed 30-day period for every plan (`Plan` has no interval/billing-cycle field of its own yet — a real per-plan cycle is future work, not a schema field invented pre-emptively here) and, mirroring every other proactive-send feature in this codebase (lapse notice, digest, deletion-resolution, staff-add), optionally notifies the business's registered merchant(s) via WhatsApp text or Meta-approved template. A new `/upgrade <plan code>` command (OWNER-only, mirroring `/addstaff`'s role gate) requests the checkout link; a new `POST /webhooks/flutterwave` route (conditionally mounted only when both `FLUTTERWAVE_SECRET_KEY` and `FLUTTERWAVE_WEBHOOK_SECRET_HASH` are configured, mirroring every other optional-credential route in `server.ts`) receives Flutterwave's `charge.completed` event and always responds 200 once the signature check passes, swallowing an unrecognized `tx_ref` as expected cross-integration webhook traffic rather than logging it as an error (Flutterwave sends this endpoint traffic for every account sharing the same webhook config, not just TradePal-initiated checkouts). Flutterwave's Standard Checkout API requires `customer.email`, which neither `Business` nor `Merchant` has anywhere in this schema — a disclosed, deliberate gap, filled with a synthetic `<phone>@invoice.tradepal.africa` placeholder built from the initiating merchant's own WhatsApp number, never shown to the merchant and never validated as deliverable.

The full backend suite passes **383/383 tests across 38 files** (up from 362/36), `npx tsc --noEmit` is clean, and `npm run build` succeeds. Five honest gaps are disclosed above, all deliberate phase-one scoping decisions rather than oversights: this is a payment-link model, not recurring/auto-charge billing (a lapsed subscription still requires the merchant to run `/upgrade` again); every plan gets a fixed 30-day period regardless of any real billing-cycle concept; the synthetic placeholder customer email is never shown to the merchant or validated as deliverable; no live Flutterwave account/sandbox verification was performed (every test mocks `fetch`, consistent with this codebase's established pattern for every other third-party integration — WhatsApp, OpenAI Whisper — none of which have had live verification either); and the admin frontend was not updated with any payment/invoice visibility (an admin can see `Plan`/`Subscription` rows today, but not `Invoice` rows or payment history).

## Phase 26 — Payment-Request Expiry Sweep (Phase 24 Gap Closure) — Build Report & Adversarial Self-Check

Recommended and accepted as the next build: Phase 24's own findings disclosed "no auto-expiry sweep exists for a stale `PENDING` `PaymentRequest` that a customer never completes." Digging in confirmed this was cheaper than a typical gap closure — `schema.prisma`'s own `PaymentRequest.status` field comment already reads `// PENDING, PAID, FAILED, EXPIRED`, meaning Phase 24 anticipated this exact state and simply never wired anything to write it. No schema or migration change was needed at all; `status` is a plain `String` column that already accepts `"EXPIRED"`.

The implementation directly mirrors `src/domain/subscriptionExpiry.ts` (Phase 6/7's hourly lapsed-subscription sweep), which is this codebase's only prior precedent for "a periodic job that scans across every business and flips a stale row's status": a new `expireStalePaymentRequests` in `src/domain/paymentRequestExpiry.ts` scans every `PENDING` `PaymentRequest` created more than `PAYMENT_REQUEST_EXPIRY_HOURS` (24) ago via the raw `PrismaClient`, then updates and audit-logs each one (`actorType: "SYSTEM"`, `actorId: PAYMENT_REQUEST_EXPIRY_JOB_NAME`, `action: "PAYMENT_REQUEST_EXPIRED"`) through `getTenantScopedClient` for its own business — same idempotency shape as `expireLapsedSubscriptions` (only `PENDING` rows are ever selected, so a second sweep run never re-expires or double-audits a row already flipped). One deliberate simplification versus its model: no notification path at all. `expireLapsedSubscriptions` optionally messages the business's own merchant about their own lapsed subscription; an expired payment *request* is the merchant's own `/paylink` attempt going stale, which the merchant already knows they sent, and Standard #9 rules out messaging the customer who'd actually be waiting on it — so there's nobody new to tell and nothing to build there.

Wiring mirrors `subscriptionExpiryWorker.ts`'s three-piece pattern exactly: a BullMQ queue module (`src/queue/paymentRequestExpiryQueue.ts`, same hourly `"0 * * * *"` cron pattern, same idempotent-registration-on-boot behavior), a standalone worker process (`src/paymentRequestExpiryWorker.ts`, registered via a new `npm run dev:payment-request-expiry-worker` script and a new `payment-request-expiry-worker` service in `docker-compose.yml`), and a `SUPER_ADMIN`-only manual-trigger admin route (`POST /admin/maintenance/expire-payment-requests`, mirroring `/admin/maintenance/expire-subscriptions`'s exact RBAC and response shape) so an operator can force the sweep synchronously instead of waiting up to an hour, and so the sweep has a Redis-free test path.

New test coverage: `tests/paymentRequestExpiry.test.ts` (new file, mirrors `tests/subscriptionExpiry.test.ts`'s shape) — a stale `PENDING` row flips to `EXPIRED` with exactly one correctly-attributed audit log row; a `PENDING` row inside the 24-hour window is left untouched; a `PAID` row is left untouched even when far older than the window (confirming a completed sale is never touched by a status-only string match); and running the sweep twice against the same stale row only expires and audits it once. ## Phase 27 — Crash/Bug Monitoring: Circuit Breaker, Degraded-Reply Handling, Email Alerting — Build Report & Adversarial Self-Check

Requested directly rather than recommended: "add a monitoring system that will monitor the system in the case of a crash or bug, it should defend and fix it automatically and send me a notification of what has been done," with email confirmed as the notification channel. Scoped deliberately to **alert + safe auto-recovery** rather than full autonomous self-healing: this system restarts a crashed process (by exiting cleanly and letting Railway's existing restart policy bring up a fresh replica — never an in-place patch) and gives a merchant a graceful degraded reply when the AI provider is unavailable, but it never edits code or redeploys anything itself.

Three new modules under `src/monitoring/`. `circuitBreaker.ts` is a small three-state (closed/open/half-open) breaker built from scratch, no library — `canAttempt()`/`recordSuccess()`/`recordFailure()`, configurable failure threshold and reset timeout, optional `onOpen`/`onClose` callbacks. `alerts.ts` is a `reportIncident(deps, { service, title, detail })` helper that always `console.error`s first (so nothing is ever silently lost, matching this codebase's existing crash-visibility baseline) and, when email deps are configured, POSTs to the Resend HTTP API (`https://api.resend.com/emails`) via raw `fetch` — the same "raw fetch, no new dependency, optional `fetchImpl` for tests" convention already used by `stt/provider.ts`, `flutterwave/client.ts`, and `whatsapp/outboundGateway.ts`. A 15-minute in-memory dedupe window (keyed by `service:title`) stops a repeating failure from flooding the inbox, and the fetch itself is wrapped in a 5-second `AbortController` timeout with all errors caught internally, so a broken or slow email provider can never hang or crash the caller. `processGuards.ts`'s `installCrashReporting(service, alerts)` registers `process.on("uncaughtException"/"unhandledRejection", ...)` handlers that report the incident by email, then `process.exit(1)` — closing a gap that predates this phase: none of the five processes in this codebase had any handler for these events before now, meaning an uncaught error anywhere would previously crash silently (or, for BullMQ's own `Worker` — an `EventEmitter` — even throw synchronously on an unhandled `"error"` event per Node's own documented contract) with no operator visibility at all. `config/monitoringEnv.ts`'s `buildAlertEmailDepsFromEnv()` mirrors the existing `buildSubscriptionExpiryOutboundGatewayFromEnv` all-or-nothing-optional-env-var pattern exactly: `ALERT_EMAIL_API_KEY`/`ALERT_EMAIL_FROM`/`ALERT_EMAIL_TO` unset means alerting is simply off (console-only), not a boot failure — every deployment and every existing test that hasn't configured this is completely unaffected.

The other half of this phase is inside `messageDispatcher.ts`, the one place in this codebase that calls an external AI vendor per inbound message. A new `parseWithCircuitBreaker` wraps the existing `parseTransactionText` call: if the breaker is open (three consecutive failures within its window), the AI call is skipped entirely and the merchant gets an immediate degraded reply ("having trouble understanding messages right now... commands like /today and /help still work") instead of waiting out a timeout against a vendor that's already down; if the breaker is closed/half-open, the call proceeds and its outcome (success/failure) is fed back into the breaker, with any actual failure also reported as an incident. The degraded path still writes a real, synthetic `AiParseLog` row (`rawModelOutput: { error: "ai_provider_unavailable" }`, not a bare `null` — `AiParseLog.rawModelOutput` is a non-nullable Prisma `Json` column, caught during design before it could become a runtime bug) rather than skipping logging outright, consistent with this codebase's own Standard #8 ("every parse is logged, success or failure"). Separately, `dispatchInboundMessage` itself is now wrapped in a try/catch/finally: any unexpected error (a DB call failing mid-dispatch, for instance) is reported as an incident and the merchant still gets a best-effort generic reply, and — regardless of success, degraded reply, or unexpected error — the originating `WebhookEvent` is now *always* moved to `PROCESSED` in a `finally` block. This closes a real, previously-unhandled failure mode: before this phase, an unexpected error partway through dispatch would leave that row permanently stuck and the merchant would get no reply at all and no indication anything had gone wrong.

All five running processes (`server.ts`, `worker.ts`, `businessDigestWorker.ts`, `subscriptionExpiryWorker.ts`, `paymentRequestExpiryWorker.ts`) now build an `alerts` dep from env (with a `console.warn` fallback exactly like every other optional credential in this codebase when unset) and call `installCrashReporting` on boot. `worker.ts` additionally builds the one `CircuitBreaker` instance (3-failure threshold, 60-second reset) threaded into `dispatchInboundMessage`'s deps, with `onOpen`/`onClose` themselves reporting an incident so a full AI-provider outage (not just a single failure) reaches the operator by email. All four BullMQ workers had their existing `.on("failed", ...)` handler upgraded to also call `reportIncident`, and all four gained a brand-new `.on("error", ...)` handler — the EventEmitter-crash gap described above — that had no equivalent anywhere in this codebase before this phase.

New test coverage: `tests/circuitBreaker.test.ts` (6 tests — closed→open on threshold, open blocks attempts until the reset timeout elapses, half-open allows exactly one probe, a successful probe closes the breaker, a failed probe re-opens it, `onOpen`/`onClose` callbacks fire with the right arguments); `tests/alerts.test.ts` (7 tests — no-op when deps are undefined, a real fetch call with the correct Resend request shape when configured, the 15-minute dedupe window suppressing a repeat within it and allowing one after it via `vi.useFakeTimers()`, a rejected/slow fetch never throwing out of `reportIncident`); and a new `describe("dispatchInboundMessage: AI-provider outage / unexpected-error handling")` block appended to `tests/messageDispatcher.test.ts` (4 tests — a thrown AI-provider error produces the degraded reply, logs the synthetic `AiParseLog` row, and still marks the webhook `PROCESSED`; three consecutive failures trip the breaker open; a subsequent call with the breaker already open skips the AI call entirely; and an unrelated unexpected error — simulated via `vi.spyOn(prisma.business, "findUniqueOrThrow").mockRejectedValueOnce(...)`, restored immediately after — still produces a generic reply and still leaves the webhook `PROCESSED`). The full backend suite passes **439 tests across 42 files**, `npx tsc -p tsconfig.json --noEmit` is clean, with zero regressions to any pre-existing success-path behavior — `alerts` and `aiCircuitBreaker` are both optional dependency-injection points, so every already-tested happy path is byte-for-byte unchanged.

Honest gaps disclosed: **no live email has actually been sent** — `ALERT_EMAIL_API_KEY`/`ALERT_EMAIL_FROM`/`ALERT_EMAIL_TO` are not yet set in this deployment's environment (no Resend, or equivalent provider, account exists yet), so `reportIncident` is exercised end-to-end only against a mocked `fetchImpl` in tests, never against the real Resend API — this is a disclosed, not silently-skipped, gap, same posture as every prior phase's disclosed live-third-party-verification gap (Flutterwave, WhatsApp templates). The "auto-fix" scope is deliberately narrow and was not re-confirmed in the requester's own words beyond the original ask: this system alerts and lets the existing process-restart/circuit-breaker mechanisms do their job, but it does not attempt any in-place code repair, config change, or redeploy — a more aggressive auto-remediation was considered and rejected as too risky to run unsupervised. No live `docker compose` verification was run for this phase specifically (unlike Phases 24–26) since nothing here required a schema or migration change; verification relied on the existing test suite plus a full clean typecheck.


## Phase 29 — Business Suspension (Platform-Moderation Ban) + Admin Credentials — Build Report & Adversarial Self-Check

Requested directly: "create a backend access for me to see users and the ability to remove user that is in violation of the platform. I want sign in credentials." Two deliverables: (1) a production `AdminUser` (SUPER_ADMIN) provisioned via the existing `scripts/createAdminUser.ts` against the live Railway Postgres instance (reached via its `DATABASE_PUBLIC_URL` proxy, not the internal-only `DATABASE_URL` other Railway services use) — no new code required, since admin auth/RBAC already existed; (2) this phase's actual build — a whole-`Business` suspension mechanism, since the two existing "removal" concepts in the codebase were both the wrong shape for "ban a business for violating platform rules": `Merchant.removedAt` (`/removestaff`) only ever soft-deactivates a single STAFF merchant, never an OWNER or the whole business, and `DeletionRequest` is customer-initiated GDPR data deletion, not admin-initiated moderation.

Schema: new `BusinessStatus` enum (`ACTIVE`/`SUSPENDED`) and `status`/`suspendedAt`/`suspensionReason` columns on `Business` (migration `20260801000000_phase29_business_suspension`, generated via `prisma migrate diff --from-schema-datamodel`/`--to-schema-datamodel` rather than `prisma migrate dev`, since no live Postgres was reachable from this environment at the time — a pure schema diff needs no shadow database or live connection). Domain layer: new `src/domain/businessModeration.ts` (`suspendBusiness`, `reinstateBusiness`, `isBusinessSuspended`, plus `BusinessAlreadySuspendedError`/`BusinessNotSuspendedError`, with `BusinessNotFoundError` reused from `planAdmin.ts` rather than redefined, so `instanceof` checks stay correct across modules) — mirrors `planAdmin.ts`'s existing shape exactly (raw-`PrismaClient` existence check → validation → write → `recordAuditLog` via a tenant-scoped client).
